Essential Travel Tips Before Booking an International Trip | Discover Holidays

Mazharul Islam
July 21, 2026
6 min read
2,840 views

Planning an international trip is one of the most exciting decisions you can make. Whether it's your first time crossing borders or you're a seasoned explorer, the preparation process can make or break your entire experience. Before you click "Book Now," there are several critical steps every traveler should take.

1. Research Your Destination Thoroughly

The first step to any successful international trip is deep research. Understand the local culture, customs, and etiquette. What's acceptable in Bangladesh may be considered offensive in certain parts of Europe or the Middle East. Check the best time to visit — weather, festivals, and peak tourist season all affect your experience and budget.

Look into safety advisories from your government's travel portal and read recent traveler reviews on forums and travel blogs. Local news sources can also give you up-to-date insights you won't find in generic travel guides.

Pro Tip: Use Google Maps to virtually walk around your destination's key areas before you arrive. It helps you get a feel for distances, nearby landmarks, and the general vibe of a neighbourhood.

2. Check Visa Requirements Early

Visa processing is often the most time-consuming part of international travel planning. Requirements change frequently — a country that was visa-on-arrival last year may now require prior approval. Always check the official embassy or consulate website for the most current requirements.

  • Apply for your visa at least 4–6 weeks before your planned departure date.
  • Gather all supporting documents: bank statements, invitation letters, hotel bookings, and return flight tickets.
  • Check if your passport is valid for at least 6 months beyond your return date — many countries require this.
  • Some countries offer e-visa options that can be processed in 24–72 hours.
"The single biggest mistake travelers make is applying for a visa too late. Give yourself buffer time — embassy processing can be unpredictable." — Discover Holidays Travel Advisory Team

3. Sort Out Your Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not optional — it's essential. A comprehensive plan covers medical emergencies, trip cancellations, lost luggage, flight delays, and even emergency evacuation. Without it, a single hospital visit abroad could cost you tens of thousands of taka.

When comparing plans, focus on the medical coverage limit and whether pre-existing conditions are covered. Look for plans with 24/7 emergency assistance hotlines.

4. Understand Currency & Money Matters

Don't wait until you're at the airport to exchange money — rates at airport counters are almost always unfavorable. Research the best exchange rates in advance. Inform your bank that you'll be traveling so your card isn't blocked for suspicious foreign transactions.

Tip: Carry a mix of cash and at least two different payment cards (Visa & Mastercard). In many countries, small vendors and local markets are cash-only.

5. Book Flights & Accommodation Strategically

Airfares fluctuate wildly. The "best" time to book is generally 6–8 weeks before departure for international flights. Flying mid-week (Tuesday or Wednesday) is typically cheaper than weekends. Flexible travel dates can save you a significant amount.

For accommodation, consider your itinerary carefully. Booking centrally-located hotels reduces transport costs and time. Read recent reviews and pay attention to comments about cleanliness, safety, and staff responsiveness.

6. Pack Smart & Light

Over-packing is the most common rookie mistake. Check your airline's baggage allowance carefully — budget carriers often have strict limits and expensive overweight fees. Create a packing list a week before departure and stick to it.

  • Roll clothes instead of folding to save space.
  • Pack a universal travel adapter.
  • Keep medications and valuables in your carry-on.
  • Take photocopies of all important documents and store them separately from originals.

7. Stay Connected & Informed

Set up international roaming or buy a local SIM card upon arrival. Download offline maps (Google Maps offline feature is excellent) and save important numbers — hotel, local emergency services, Bangladesh embassy — before you go. Register with your country's embassy in your destination country for emergency notifications.

Final Thoughts

International travel is one of life's greatest joys, but it rewards those who prepare. Start your research early, handle documentation first, and leave nothing to last-minute.
